# Literature Map: Subject Individuation and Causal Boundaries

## Purpose

This document maps research that intersects with, supports, constrains, or contradicts the working hypothesis that subject individuation may be implemented by a dynamically maintained causal-access boundary.

The target is not to prove a metaphysics of consciousness. It is to identify physical and computational variables that might explain why some states belong to one bounded perspective rather than another.

The staged implementation plan is in [subject-boundary-research-plan.md](subject-boundary-research-plan.md).

The central discipline is:

phenomenal existence != subject individuation != functional integration

**AD-1** denotes the subject-individuation / causal-boundary hypothesis defined in [subject-individuation.md](subject-individuation.md): conditional on phenomenal experience, subject boundaries may depend partly on causal integration, information access, self-modeling, memory, and control rather than automatically matching anatomical or software-process boundaries. It does not claim that those features are sufficient for consciousness.

A useful evidentiary ladder remains:

1. information transfer;
2. representational sharing;
3. distributed functional integration;
4. overlapping functional perspective;
5. shared phenomenal token.

No lower level entails a higher one.

## 1. Split-brain research: unity is probably multidimensional

Pinto, de Haan, and Lamme (2017) challenge the classical picture that callosotomy simply creates two conscious subjects. They argue that at least some split-brain patients may remain one conscious agent despite divided perceptual processing.

Schechter and Bayne (2021) sharpen the problem by separating unity of agency from unity of experience. They conclude that behavioral unity does not settle phenomenal unity and suggest that split-brain consciousness may be only partially unified.

Lopez (2025) develops a layered-unity model. Split-brain subjects can identify stimuli separately while failing to compare them across visual fields; Lopez argues that local experiential layers may be disunified while more global layers remain unified.

### Implication for AD-1

A subject boundary should not initially be represented by one scalar. At minimum, measure separate boundaries for:

- perceptual content;
- cross-content comparison and access;
- agency and control;
- autobiographical memory;
- metacognition;
- preference and valence.

A successful individuation theory should explain when these boundaries coincide and when they dissociate.

## 2. Bodily self-consciousness and interoception: perspective is actively constructed

Brunello et al. (2025) find partially distinct and overlapping neural substrates for body ownership and agency across 56 neuroimaging studies. Candia-Rivera et al. (2024) review evidence that bodily self-awareness is malleable and deeply coupled to interoceptive networks, and propose network-physiology approaches spanning brain and body.

### Implication for AD-1

The boundary of a subject may not be purely cortical. Candidate variables should include:

- interoceptive coupling;
- sensorimotor prediction;
- agency attribution;
- multisensory binding;
- brain-body network structure.

A causal-boundary theory that ignores embodiment may recover information-processing boundaries while missing the physical basis of self-location and ownership.

## 3. Markov blankets and active inference: a formal boundary candidate, not an identity claim

Kirchhoff et al. (2018) model biological autonomy using Markov blankets: statistical boundaries that separate internal and external states through sensory and active states. They emphasize nested, self-sustaining boundaries across scales.

Friston et al. (2021) extend Markov-blanket analysis to nested parcels and particles in brain organization.

However, Raja et al. (2021) argue that Markov-blanket and free-energy formalisms are often overextended. A statistical conditional-independence boundary does not by itself explain biological perception, action, cognition, or a phenomenal self.

### Implication for AD-1

Markov-blanket structure is a candidate predictor of individuation, not a definition of it.

The relevant experiment is not "does the system have a Markov blanket?" but:

> Does the best-fitting blanket move when independently measured boundaries of agency, memory, metacognition, self-location, or valence move?

If not, blanket structure is too generic for the target.

## 4. Information-theoretic autonomy: quantify preferential self-dependence

Bertschinger et al. (2008) propose an information-theoretic measure of autonomy based on how strongly a system's next state depends on its own prior state after conditioning on environmental history.

This directly intersects the causal-access hypothesis because it attempts to quantify the extent to which a candidate system has temporally self-determining dynamics rather than simply reflecting its environment.

### Implication for AD-1

Autonomy measures should be evaluated as boundary scores, especially under coupling sweeps. But they require careful handling of bidirectional influence and synergistic system-environment interactions, which the original authors themselves identify as complications.

## 5. Causal emergence and Integrated Information Decomposition: the closest formal overlap

Rosas et al. (2020) provide a data-driven account of causal emergence in which a macroscale feature can have predictive information about a system's future that is unavailable from individual microscale components.

Luppi et al. (2021) use Integrated Information Decomposition (PhiID) to distinguish forms of emergence, including causal decoupling. They explicitly propose that an individuated self may correspond to persistent macroscale dynamics whose predictive power cannot be reduced to the constituent parts.

### Implication for AD-1

This is currently the closest formal match to the hypothesis.

A high-priority candidate measure is therefore:

> persistent causal decoupling of a macroscale representation from its constituent parts.

The crucial test is whether causal-decoupling boundaries align with independently measured functional self-boundaries better than generic complexity, synchrony, task performance, or anatomical grouping.

## 6. Integrated Information Theory: an explicit competing boundary model

IIT 4.0 identifies conscious substrates with maximally irreducible cause-effect structures. Its exclusion postulate selects maxima among overlapping candidate substrates; it does not require non-overlapping candidate substrates to collapse into one global winner, so multiple non-overlapping maximal complexes may coexist (Albantakis et al. 2023).

This overlaps strongly with AD-1 because both treat system boundaries as causal rather than merely anatomical.

It also creates a useful tension. IIT's exclusion principle favors definite maximally integrated complexes, whereas the present subject-individuation program allows the possibility of layered or partially overlapping functional boundaries.

### Discriminating prediction

Under a graded coupling sweep between two systems:

- an exclusion-style model permits multiple non-overlapping maximal complexes when A and B remain sufficiently separate; as coupling changes, maxima among overlapping candidate substrates may shift, including a possible transition to an A+B complex;
- a layered-boundary model allows different forms of unity to cross the inter-system boundary at different coupling strengths;
- an ordinary communication model predicts improved information transfer without constitutive boundary migration.

This should be treated as a head-to-head empirical comparison, not as a presumption that any model is correct.

## 7. Enaction and precarious autonomy: integration may be insufficient without self-maintenance

Beer and Di Paolo (2023) characterize enactive autonomy using operational closure plus precariousness: a network of mutually supporting processes whose organization must continually maintain itself against possible disintegration.

This is a strong challenge to any theory that equates subjecthood with information integration alone.

### Implication for AD-1

Add a self-maintenance dimension to machine experiments.

Two systems may be highly integrated yet lack a system-relative distinction between states that preserve versus degrade their continued organization. If self-boundary phenomena appear only when integration is coupled to persistent self-maintenance, that would favor an enactive/autonomy account over a pure integration account.

This can be tested using low-severity artificial variables; no distress-like state is required.

## 8. Perturbational complexity and central thalamic orchestration: causal organization matters, but state level is not boundary location

Casali et al. (2013) introduced perturbational complexity as a way to quantify differentiated, integrated responses to direct cortical perturbation across conscious and unconscious conditions. Luppi et al. (2024) further show in macaques that anesthesia collapses distributed hierarchical cortical organization and that specific central-thalamic stimulation can reverse several distributed signatures together with behavioral arousal.

### Implication for AD-1

Perturbation is methodologically important because it measures effective causal organization rather than passive correlation.

However, global complexity and arousal are not subject-boundary measures. The research program should adapt perturbational logic into boundary mapping:

- perturb node A;
- quantify propagation into candidate subsystem B;
- identify where recurrent influence becomes negligible or qualitatively changes;
- compare that boundary with self-relevant functional boundaries.

## 9. Hyperscanning and brain-to-brain interfaces: critical negative controls

BrainNet demonstrated direct non-invasive brain-to-brain communication among three people using EEG, TMS, and an Internet channel. The receiver integrated decisions from two senders without implying that the participants became one subject.

Wearable OPM-MEG now permits naturalistic two-person hyperscanning with millisecond-scale neural recording (Holmes et al. 2023).

Schilbach and Redcay (2025) review evidence that inter-brain synchrony supports social interaction and communication. Jiang, Zou, and Yu (2026) meta-analyze EEG hyperscanning and find stronger inter-brain synchrony during social interaction, while emphasizing task heterogeneity, shared stimulation, behavioral synchrony, and analytic confounds. Berni et al. (2026) similarly find substantial methodological heterogeneity across fMRI hyperscanning studies and note that only a minority of paradigms combine the features needed to exploit truly reciprocal interaction.

### Implication for AD-1

Synchrony, communication bandwidth, and mutual prediction are not sufficient markers of a shared subject.

Any proposed boundary metric must discriminate:

- common input;
- behavioral entrainment;
- ordinary communication;
- direct brain-to-brain information transfer;
- constitutive distributed representation.

These are mandatory negative controls.

## 10. AI-consciousness methodology: avoid generic capacity markers and mimicry

Butlin et al. (2026) emphasize the value of internal variants of consciousness indicators because behavior can mimic relevant signatures. Taschereau-Dumouchel et al. (2026) warn that many proposed markers of consciousness may primarily track general information-processing capacity rather than subjective experience.

### Implication for AD-1

The individuation program should make the analogous distinction:

- generic capability;
- generic integration;
- self-relevant integration;
- constitutive distributed representation;
- functional subject-boundary convergence;
- phenomenal inference.

A metric that merely rises with model size, task success, or communication bandwidth is not an individuation metric.

## 11. Synthesis: what the literature changes

The literature supports a stronger and more constrained version of AD-1.

### Revised working model

A candidate functional subject boundary is not simply the edge of high connectivity.

It is better treated as a multidimensional, dynamically maintained partition characterized by some combination of:

- preferential causal self-dependence;
- macroscale causal emergence or decoupling;
- recurrent accessibility;
- sensorimotor and interoceptive anchoring;
- self-model attribution;
- temporal persistence and memory;
- system-relative self-maintenance;
- metacognitive access;
- preference and valence scope.

### Major competing explanations

1. **Communication-only:** coupling transfers information but each subsystem reconstructs and owns its own states.
2. **Layered functional individuation:** different forms of unity have different boundaries.
3. **Causal-emergence boundary:** an emergent macroscale entity is identified by persistent irreducible predictive structure.
4. **Exclusion/maximal-complex boundary:** maxima among overlapping candidate substrates define the relevant complexes at a given grain; multiple non-overlapping maximal complexes may coexist.
5. **Enactive-autonomy boundary:** individuation requires operational closure plus self-maintaining/precarious organization.
6. **Statistical-boundary account:** Markov-blanket structure captures the relevant internal/external partition.
7. **Hybrid account:** several of the above identify different necessary components.

The research program should be designed to make these models disagree.

## Strongest current lessons

- Unity is likely layered rather than all-or-nothing.
- Anatomical boundaries are not automatically functional boundaries.
- Statistical boundaries are not automatically phenomenal boundaries.
- Synchrony and communication are weak evidence for individuation.
- Integration without self-maintenance may be insufficient.
- Causal perturbation is more informative than passive correlation.
- PhiID-style causal decoupling is a particularly strong formal candidate.
- Subject-boundary evidence should converge across independent dimensions rather than depend on one metric.
- Phenomenal identity remains a separate inference even if a distributed functional subject is established.
