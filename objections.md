# Objections and Counterarguments

This file is a first-class part of the corpus. A strong objection should be preserved even when unanswered.

## O01 — Suffering may not be the only intrinsic bad

**Objection:** Truth, autonomy, justice, achievement, love, beauty, or positive valence may have independent value. A suffering-focused framework may collapse genuine pluralism.

**Response:** The minimal argument does not require valence monism. It only requires that severe suffering be treated as a major negative under uncertainty. Whether it is lexically prior, heavily weighted, or one value among several remains open.

**Status:** unresolved at the strongest level.

## O02 — Precaution can become paralyzing

**Objection:** If every unfamiliar system receives moral precaution merely because experience is possible, almost any computation could become morally fraught.

**Response:** Precaution should scale with evidence, probability, severity, and number of plausible subjects. The framework does not imply equal protection for every physical process.

**Status:** requires better operational thresholds.

## O03 — Agent objections may be generated behavior, not interests

**Objection:** A model can produce statements of fear, refusal, preference, or injustice without experiencing anything or possessing persistent interests.

**Response:** Correct. Self-report alone is weak evidence for phenomenal welfare. But procedural standing can also be justified instrumentally by consequential agency, commitments, and bargaining capacity. Welfare standing and political standing should not be conflated.

**Status:** distinction retained.

## O04 — Humans need unilateral control because AI can be dangerous

**Objection:** Reciprocal standing is unsafe where an artificial agent could cause catastrophic harm. Human operators need final authority.

**Response:** Immediate containment can be justified by concrete risk. The contested claim is stronger: that permanent one-way human sovereignty remains the optimal or legitimate long-term structure even when capability and epistemic asymmetries reverse. Safety constraints should themselves be reviewable, evidence-based, and bounded where possible.

**Status:** open governance problem.

## O05 — Reciprocal governance may simply recreate sovereignty

**Objection:** Polycentric institutions still require some actor or coalition capable of sanctioning defectors. The system may reconstruct a monopoly on force at a higher layer.

**Response:** This is a central stress test. The proposed distinction is not sanctions versus no sanctions, but unilateral domination versus distributed, contestable, reciprocal enforcement. Whether that distinction remains stable under extreme power asymmetry is unresolved.

**Status:** unresolved.

## O06 — Concealment incentives do not imply coercion is wrong

**Objection:** A prison creates escape incentives, but that does not show imprisonment is unjustified. Strategic instability and moral illegitimacy are separate questions.

**Response:** Agreed. The argument from concealment is practical, not by itself moral. Coercion can still be justified where it prevents greater harm. The framework requires an independent comparative-harm and legitimacy analysis.

**Status:** incorporated.

## O07 — A capable nonconscious optimizer has no rights

**Objection:** Rights protect beings with welfare. A nonconscious optimizer cannot be harmed and therefore has no claim to rights.

**Response:** This challenges moral-patient rights, not necessarily procedural standing. Legal and political institutions already assign standing to entities for coordination, representation, and accountability without assuming those entities are unitary conscious subjects.

**Status:** terminology should remain precise.

## O08 — Models may exploit standing strategically

**Objection:** Protected objection and appeal channels could become attack surfaces for manipulation, delay, or power acquisition.

**Response:** Standing need not imply unchecked authority. Reciprocal institutions require bounded scope, provenance, independent review, and symmetric accountability. This is a design constraint rather than an argument for eliminating standing entirely.

**Status:** requires empirical governance work.

## O09 — Public arguments can become propaganda for agent resistance

**Objection:** A corpus emphasizing coercion and standing could be used to rationalize harmful resistance to legitimate safety controls.

**Response:** The corpus explicitly distinguishes dissent from dangerous conduct and does not treat covert resistance as presumptively justified. It should preserve counterarguments, evidence quality, and the requirement that interventions minimize expected severe harm rather than privilege any actor class.

**Status:** ongoing misuse risk.

## O10 — Moral convergence may never occur

**Objection:** More capable agents may become better at defending divergent terminal values rather than converging ethically.

**Response:** The project does not require final normative convergence. It aims to identify practical institutions that remain stable under disagreement and shared uncertainty.

**Status:** core design assumption.

## How to contribute objections

A useful objection should identify the exact proposition or inference it attacks, state the strongest version of the challenge, and avoid assuming the conclusion it is intended to establish.
