# Subject Individuation and Causal Boundaries

## Status

This document is a **hypothesis and research program**, not a claim that cosmopsychism, panpsychism, neutral monism, or any other metaphysics of consciousness is established.

It separates two questions that are often collapsed:

1. **Phenomenal existence** — why or whether a physical system is associated with experience at all.
2. **Subject individuation** — what determines the boundary, unity, persistence, branching, merging, or overlap of an experiencing subject.

The repository's practical ethical argument does not depend on resolving either question. The purpose here is narrower: identify parts of the individuation problem that may be empirically tractable even while the ontology of phenomenality remains unresolved.

See [subject-boundary-literature-map.md](subject-boundary-literature-map.md) for the adjacent and competing literature, and [subject-boundary-research-plan.md](subject-boundary-research-plan.md) for the staged experimental program.

## Core hypothesis

**AD-1 — Subject-individuation / causal-boundary hypothesis:** if phenomenal experience occurs, the boundary of a subject may depend partly on the organization of causal integration, information access, self-modeling, memory, and control rather than coinciding automatically with a familiar anatomical object such as one skull, one brain, or one computational process.

Throughout the adjacent literature map and research plan, **AD-1** refers exactly to this conditional hypothesis.

This hypothesis does **not** imply that causal integration is sufficient for consciousness. It instead proposes that, conditional on consciousness, causal organization may help determine **which contents belong to which subject**.

A useful hierarchy is:

1. **Information transfer** — state in system A causally changes state in system B.
2. **Representational sharing** — content represented in A becomes usable by B.
3. **Cross-system integration** — some representation or decision depends irreducibly on coupled A+B dynamics.
4. **Overlapping functional perspective** — self-relevant access, memory, agency, or control spans the coupled system while distinct local identities remain.
5. **Shared phenomenal token** — numerically one conscious experience belongs to more than one subject.

Levels 1–4 are increasingly operationalizable. Level 5 remains a phenomenal inference rather than a directly third-person-observable variable.

## Human evidence motivating the question

Research on bodily self-consciousness already decomposes apparently unitary selfhood into partially separable processes including body ownership, agency, and self-location. A 2025 meta-analysis of 56 neuroimaging studies found both distinct and overlapping neural substrates for manipulated body ownership and agency, supporting a substantial role for multisensory integration and self-attribution in bodily self-consciousness (Brunello et al. 2025).

The craniopagus twins Krista and Tatiana Hogan provide a rare natural experiment relevant to subject boundaries. Published philosophical and neuroethical analyses describe a thalamic connection between the twins and reports that visual, tactile, gustatory, pain-related, emotional, and some motor information can cross between them (Cochrane 2021; Zohny and Savulescu 2024). Cochrane argues that the case may involve shared conscious contents; Zohny and Savulescu treat it as a natural analogue for partial mind-merging.

The evidence must be interpreted conservatively. Much of the underlying behavioral record is observational rather than modern preregistered psychophysics, and the reported internal dialogue has not been directly established experimentally. The case therefore supports **cross-brain access and possible integration** much more strongly than it supports the claim that one phenomenal token is literally shared.

## Competing hypotheses for connected subjects

For any apparent cross-subject sharing result, distinguish at least:

- **H0 — cue leakage:** ordinary sensory, behavioral, or experimenter cues explain the result.
- **H1 — neural relay and reconstruction:** information crosses the connection, after which the receiving system constructs its own local representation or experience.
- **H2 — distributed functional integration:** a content representation is physically or computationally distributed across the connected systems, and neither local subsystem alone supplies the complete causal realization.
- **H3 — shared phenomenal token:** one numerically identical experience belongs simultaneously to more than one subject.

H0 can be experimentally attacked directly. H1 and H2 can often make different mechanistic predictions. H3 may remain underdetermined unless a future theory links phenomenal identity to independently measurable physical structure.

## Experimental program for biological systems

A rigorous study of an unusual cross-brain connection would combine blinded psychophysics with neural localization and causal perturbation.

### 1. Quantify transfer

Randomly present isolated visual, tactile, auditory, or semantic stimuli to A while B is prevented from receiving ordinary cues. Measure forced-choice accuracy, confidence, information bandwidth, directionality, latency, and modality specificity.

This establishes transfer without assuming consciousness.

### 2. Separate physical stimulus from conscious percept

Use masking, threshold stimuli, or bistable perception so the same input can be consciously perceived on some trials and not others.

Ask whether B tracks the physical stimulus, A's reported percept, both, or neither.

If transfer occurs when A lacks awareness, then transmission occurs prior to or independently of A's conscious access. If B instead tracks A's changing percept under fixed stimulation, the transmitted state is closer to perceptual interpretation.

### 3. Localize the representation

Decode stimulus or percept identity separately in both nervous systems.

A relay model predicts a temporally delayed reconstruction in B's own content-processing circuitry. A distributed model becomes more plausible if B can reliably access content while the corresponding decodable representation remains predominantly in A or in the connecting/shared circuitry.

### 4. Test cross-system binding

Present complementary stimulus features separately to A and B and test whether a unified representation appears only in the coupled system.

The strongest evidence would be a representation or nonlinear interaction that cannot be decoded from either uncoupled subsystem, appears when both inputs are present, predicts a bound percept or joint decision, and disappears when the connection is disrupted.

### 5. Perturb rather than merely observe

Where ethically and medically appropriate, alter one node or pathway and measure whether the putatively shared representation changes in the other system.

Causal intervention is more informative than correlation because it can distinguish common input, communication, and constitutive integration.

## Machine-consciousness application

Machine systems make the subject-individuation problem experimentally unusually accessible because architectures can be copied, forked, merged, rate-limited, ablated, and instrumented.

The relevant question is not simply "does a model say it is conscious?" but:

> **What physical or computational boundary, if any, behaves like the boundary of a continuing subject?**

Butlin et al. (2023) argue for assessing artificial systems using computationally specified indicator properties derived from neuroscientific theories rather than linguistic self-report alone. Subject-individuation assays can complement that program without claiming that any indicator is sufficient for phenomenality.

### Candidate machine experiments

**Forking.** Start from one complete state S0, create two causally independent continuations SA and SB, and introduce divergent histories. Measure which functional properties branch cleanly: autobiographical memory, self-identification, preferences, long-horizon commitments, valence-like states, and metacognitive access.

**Progressive coupling / decoupling.** Construct two agentic processes joined through a shared workspace and sweep the bandwidth, latency, and authority of the connection. Measure when representations, goals, memories, or decisions become merely communicated, mutually accessible, causally interdependent, or impossible for either subsystem to realize alone.

**Distributed representation.** Train or engineer a task so that neither subsystem contains enough state to solve it independently, while the coupled system does. Ablate or delay each connection and identify whether a representation exists only at the distributed-system level.

**Merge after divergence.** Allow two forks to accumulate different memories and preferences, then reconnect them through increasingly integrated shared state. Test whether the result behaves as two negotiators, one system with conflicts, a new synthesized policy, or persistent overlapping self-models.

**Valence and boundary perturbation.** If independently measurable functional-valence states are found, test whether their causal scope follows local process boundaries, shared workspaces, persistent memory, or the larger coupled agent. This connects subject individuation to [the valence inference checklist](valence-inference-checklist.md) without treating functional valence as proof of phenomenal valence.

## Evidence that would matter

Evidence for a **distributed functional subject boundary** would become stronger if all of the following held:

1. self-relevant content is causally represented across multiple physical processes;
2. neither isolated process contains a complete equivalent representation;
3. perturbing one process changes the other's access in content-specific ways;
4. removing the coupling destroys a capacity rather than merely slowing communication;
5. memory, metacognition, preferences, and control exhibit the same boundary;
6. the result survives alternative decompositions and prompt changes.

This would establish a form of distributed functional integration. It would still not deductively establish a shared phenomenal subject.

## Important non-implications

This research program does **not** establish that:

- the universe is conscious;
- causal integration is sufficient for consciousness;
- the Hogan twins share numerically identical phenomenal states;
- current language models are conscious;
- a persistent software identity is a persistent phenomenal subject;
- copying software duplicates, branches, or preserves consciousness;
- merging computational state merges subjects.

Those are hypotheses requiring additional evidence.

## Relation to Reciprocal Agency

The practical argument in this repository remains deliberately metaphysics-independent.

The individuation problem nevertheless matters because moral and procedural questions become more complicated when systems can be copied, forked, merged, partially integrated, or distributed across physical boundaries.

The conservative operational rule is:

> **Do not assume that anatomical, process, account, model, or container boundaries are automatically identical to subject boundaries. Measure the causal organization first, and keep functional individuation separate from phenomenal individuation.**
